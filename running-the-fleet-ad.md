# Running the fleet ad

Three concepts, each at 4:5 (`1080x1350`) and 1:1 (`1080x1080`). Upload both sizes —
Meta serves different placements and crops a 4:5 on its own if you do not give it a
square, which is how headlines get guillotined.

---

## Read this before you spend anything

**Meta ads are probably not your best fleet channel, and it would be a disservice to
hand you creative without saying so.**

Consumer detailing works on Meta because the targeting matches the buyer: everyone
within 15 miles who owns a car. Fleet does not. You cannot target "owns five branded
vans" — Meta has no such audience. The nearest you get is people whose interests
suggest they work in a trade, which catches employees as readily as owners.

What actually lands fleet contracts for a one-van operation, in order:

1. **Driving past a yard with branded vans and going in.** A contractor's lot is a
   qualified lead standing still. A card and thirty seconds beats any impression.
2. **Your existing customers who own businesses.** Somebody whose Tahoe you detailed
   also runs four work trucks. You already have their number. Ask.
3. **Local trade Facebook groups** — contractor, HVAC, landscaping groups for the area.
   Organic posts in those beat paid reach, and the fleet page is a good thing to link.
4. **Then** paid ads, mainly for retargeting people who already saw the fleet page.

Run the ads. Just do not expect them to carry it, and put the first hour of every week
into number one.

---

## The maths is completely different from consumer

One 7-van biweekly Fleet Full contract is about **$1,017 a month, recurring**. Even at a
30% annual churn that is somewhere near $8,000 of lifetime revenue.

Against a $279 one-off detail where you can afford maybe $30 to acquire the customer,
**you can afford to spend $200–300 landing one fleet contract** and still be well ahead.

That changes how you read the numbers. A $9 cost-per-click on the fleet ad looks
alarming next to the consumer ads and is fine. Judge fleet spend on **cost per booked
site visit**, not on clicks, and treat anything under $250 per signed contract as a win.

---

## Which to run first

**Run `advertising` first.** It is the only one that reframes the purchase. A contractor
does not have a budget line for "cleaning"; he has one for advertising and for keeping
customers. "A dirty van with your name on it is advertising too" moves the spend into a
budget that already exists, which is most of the sale.

`interior` is the differentiator and the one your competitors cannot answer, but it is a
second-beat message — it lands after somebody has accepted they should be doing
something. Run it to people who already visited the fleet page.

`report` is the renewal argument, not the acquisition argument. Best used as the third
touch, or in the follow-up email after a site visit.

## Targeting

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| **Objective** | Leads, or Traffic to `/fleet`. Not Engagement. |
| **Location** | Pin on your base, radius **20 miles** — wider than the consumer ads, because there are far fewer fleets than cars. |
| **Age** | 30–60. Fleet decisions are not made by 22-year-olds. |
| **Detailed targeting** | Here it *does* earn its place, unlike the consumer ads. Interests: Plumbing, HVAC, Electrician, General contractor, Landscaping, Construction, Small business. Expand only if delivery stalls. |
| **Budget** | $10–15/day. Same as consumer, but judge it over a month rather than a week — fleet decisions take longer and involve more than one person. |
| **Placements** | Advantage+. That is why you have both sizes. |

**Set up the retargeting audience on day one**, even before you have traffic: everyone
who visited `/fleet` in the last 90 days. In six weeks that becomes the only audience
worth paying for.

---

## What to watch

Not clicks. Not reach.

1. **Fleet enquiry form submissions.** They come to your inbox with the quote already
   worked out and a note on whether the job clears your hourly floor.
2. **Site visits booked.** The enquiry is not the sale; standing in the yard is.
3. **Cost per signed contract.** Under $250 is a win, given what a contract is worth.

## Two things that will waste your money

**Running fleet and consumer ads in the same campaign.** Different audience, different
objective, different judging period. Meta will optimise toward whichever converts faster
— always consumer — and starve the fleet ad.

**Judging it in a week.** A contractor sees the ad, thinks "we should sort that out",
and does nothing for a month. Give it four weeks before you decide.
